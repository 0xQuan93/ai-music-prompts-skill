# Music-making basics (for our WaveWarZ club tracks)

Research notes, 25 Nov 2026. The basics of how a track is built, tied to the house and Jersey club tracks we make, and to the Suno prompt words that control each part. Sources at the end; tutorial and forum sources are marked as softer evidence.

## 1. The six building blocks

Every track is these six things. Change one at a time and you hear what it does.

| Block | What it is | In our tracks | Suno prompt words that control it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Rhythm** | the drums and the pattern they play | four-on-the-floor kick (house), triplet kick bounce (Jersey club), offbeat hats, claps | "four-on-the-floor kick", "Jersey club triplet kick bounce", "shuffled hats", BPM |
| **Bass** | the low part that locks with the kick | rubbery sub, growling mid-bass | "rubbery sub bass", "growling mid-bass", "tight mono low end" |
| **Harmony** | the chords: the mood (minor = dark/tense, major = bright) | almost every track came back in a minor key (G, D, A#, F, B minor) | "minor-key", "uplifting major chords", "dark", "euphoric" |
| **Melody** | the tune you remember: a riff or the vocal line | siren riff (Round B), synth stab, sung hook | "one memorable four-note synth riff", "sung lead vocal" |
| **Structure** | what plays when, in blocks of bars | intro, build, drop, breakdown, outro | section tags: `[Intro]`, `[Build]`, `[Drop]`, `[Break]`, `[Outro]` |
| **Sound and mix** | the tone of each part and how they sit together | dry vocal, punchy drums, centered bass | "dry close lead vocal", "dry punchy drums", "not over-limited" |

## 2. Time: beats, bars, phrases

- **Beat:** one tap of your foot. **BPM** = beats per minute.
- **Bar:** 4 beats. At 128 BPM a bar is 1.9 s; at 140 BPM, 1.7 s.
- **Phrase:** dance music moves in blocks of **8, 16 and 32 bars**. Small changes land every 8 bars, bigger ones every 16 (forum rule of thumb).
- **Why it matters:** DJs mix on phrase boundaries ("phrase mixing"), so a club track's intro is usually a multiple of 16 bars, often adding one new element every 32 beats (8 bars).

| BPM | 16 bars | 32 bars |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 128 | 30 s | 60 s |
| 140 | 27 s | 55 s |

## 3. House basics

- **Tempo:** about 120 to 130 BPM.
- **Groove:** a kick on every beat, claps or snare on beats 2 and 4, open hats on the offbeats (between the kicks).
- **Typical shape:** intro 16 to 32 bars, build or breakdown 8 to 16, drop 16 to 32, changes on 8 or 16-bar lines.
- **Arranging trick:** the "subtractive" method. Build one full 8 or 16-bar loop with everything in it, copy it across the timeline, then remove parts to make the intro, breakdown and outro.

## 4. Jersey club basics

- **Where it's from:** Newark, New Jersey, growing out of Baltimore club around 1999 to 2000 (DJ Tameil, the Brick City Club scene).
- **Tempo:** about 130 to 140 BPM.
- **The kick:** the signature is a bouncing kick pattern rather than a steady one. Sources describe it differently: one gives a five-kick pattern on steps 1, 5, 9, 12 and 15 of a 16-step grid; Native Instruments describes two quarter-note kicks, two dotted eighths, then an eighth. Both land as the "bounce" we've been asking Suno for.
- **Signature sounds:** the bed squeak (from a chopped 2004 sample), vocal chops, call-and-response. Producers treat the squeak as percussion, and many strong tracks use it lightly or skip it.
- **Why it suits WaveWarZ:** it's built for crowds and battles, it sits close to house tempo, and the bounce makes a drop feel like a hit.

## 5. Song basics (lyrics and vocals)

- **Hook:** the line people remember. Short, singable, sung at the chorus.
- **Verse:** tells the story, with different words each time. This is where the WaveWarZ, The ZAO, blockchain and open-source lines go.
- **Pre-chorus:** a short lift that sets up the hook.
- **Repetition:** some is the point of a hook; too much feels like a loop. Round C showed Suno adds repeats on its own, so write fewer, and cut extras after export.
- **Pronunciation:** spell words the way they're sung. "The ZAO" is written **"The Zow"** in lyrics from Round D on.

## 6. How this changes our next prompts

1. Pick the genre's tempo first (house 124 to 128, Jersey club 135 to 140).
2. One rhythm idea, one bass sound, one memorable riff, one mood (key).
3. Write real verses; the hook twice.
4. Ask for intros and outros in 16 or 32 bars, and expect to fix the exact length in a DAW.
5. Phonetic spelling for names: WaveWarZ, The Zow.

## 7. Free places to start making music yourself

- **Carnegie Hall, Digital Music Production** - free; loop-based, has an arrangement unit and a free sample pack; uses the free SoundTrap app.
- **Guy Michelmore, Foundations of Making Music (Skillshare)** - free micro-course on harmony, melody, rhythm, texture and structure; works with any DAW.
- **Audiotool** - a free browser DAW used by beginner beat courses.
- **A good habit:** take one of our Suno clips, export the stems, and rebuild a 16-bar loop from them in a DAW. You learn every block from the inside.
