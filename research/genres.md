# Genre guide (for WaveWarZ rounds)

Research notes, 25 Nov 2026. A follow-on to [`music-basics.md`](music-basics.md): what defines each genre (tempo, rhythm, signature sound), a starter line for a Suno style prompt, and how it could fit WaveWarZ. Tempo alone doesn't define a genre; at 128 BPM a track could be house, techno or trance. The drum pattern, bass and arrangement decide it.

Ranges marked *general knowledge* come from common producer practice, not from the sources searched here; check them against a reference track.

## Quick table

| Genre | BPM | Rhythm | Signature sound | WaveWarZ fit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| House | 120-130 | kick every beat, offbeat hats | warm bass, claps on 2 and 4 | the base we've been using |
| Jersey club | 130-140 | bouncing kick pattern | bed squeak, vocal chops, call and response | battle bounce on drops |
| Techno | 120-140 (often ~130) | relentless four-on-the-floor | mechanical, repetitive, built for long DJ sets | workout / long sets |
| Trance | 135-145 (range 125-150) | four-on-the-floor | long builds, emotional breakdowns, euphoric drops | victory / anthem moments |
| Dubstep | ~140, felt as half-time | snare on beat 3 only | heavy wobble or growl bass, big drops | boss battles |
| UK garage / 2-step | 128-138 | skipped kicks (2-step), shuffled hats | swung groove, chopped vocals, warm bass | groovy, crowd-friendly |
| Drum and bass | 160-180 *general knowledge* | fast breakbeats, snare on 2 and 4 | rolling sub, chopped breaks | high-intensity cardio |
| Amapiano | 108-115 (Bacardi up to 116-122) | slow swung groove | log drum (pitched, gliding bass-drum), Rhodes chords, shakers | the slow-burn, cool-down chapter |
| Baile funk | ~130 (weak evidence) | tamborzão hand-drum syncopation | Afro-Brazilian percussion, vocal chops | party energy, crowd chants |
| Drift / Brazilian phonk | 120-140 (some packs 165+) | hard kick, heavy 808 | 808 cowbell lead, dark atmosphere, clipped bass | gym and car energy, villain theme |
| Baltimore club | 125-135 *general knowledge* | broken kick pattern, breakbeat chops | the parent of Jersey club; vocal chops, call and response | origin story |
| Hip-hop / trap | 130-150 trap, felt as half-time *general knowledge* | 808 kicks, rolling hi-hats, snare on 3 | sliding 808 bass, rap or melodic vocal | verses, storytelling |

## Notes by genre

### Techno
Mechanical and repetitive by design; it serves the DJ and the dance floor more than a pop song structure. Change comes slowly, a layer at a time.
**Suno starter:** `Peak-time techno, 132 BPM, relentless four-on-the-floor kick, hypnotic synth loop that changes every 16 bars, minimal vocal.`

### Trance
Long builds, emotional breakdowns and euphoric drops; modern trance sits at the higher end, toward 140+. Subgenres: hard trance 140 to 180, psytrance faster still with rhythms that change every 8 bars.
**Suno starter:** `Uplifting trance, 140 BPM, rolling bassline, soaring supersaw lead, long breakdown with emotional chords, euphoric drop.`

### Dubstep
Around 140 BPM but felt at half-time: the snare lands on beat 3, not 2 and 4, so it feels like 70. Some dubstep uses a four-on-the-floor groove instead.
**Suno starter:** `Heavy dubstep, 140 BPM half-time, snare on beat three, growling wobble bass, big drop after a silent bar.`

### UK garage / 2-step
About 130 BPM. The 2-step pattern drops kicks from the straight four (often landing on steps 1 and 11 of a 16-step bar) and swings the hats. Speeding up a house track doesn't make it garage; the syncopation does.
**Suno starter:** `UK garage 2-step, 132 BPM, skipping kick pattern, swung shuffled hats, warm organ bass, smooth soulful vocal.`

### Drum and bass
Fast breakbeats, usually 160 to 180 BPM *(general knowledge)*. Half-time DnB keeps the tempo but puts one snare on beat 3.
**Suno starter:** `Liquid drum and bass, 174 BPM, rolling chopped breakbeat, deep sub bass, bright pads, clear sung vocal.`

### Amapiano
South African; 108 to 115 BPM, 112 is a common start. The log drum is the defining sound, somewhere between a kick, an 808, a synth bass and real log percussion; its punch comes from the glide between notes, not volume. Rhodes chords, shakers and swung percussion around it. Producers warn against speeding it up to fix an empty loop; fix the percussion and swing instead.
**Suno starter:** `Amapiano, 112 BPM, swung groove, gliding log drum bassline, jazzy Rhodes chords, airy shakers, laid-back vocal.`

### Baile funk and Brazilian phonk
Baile funk's tamborzão beat uses hand drums and Afro-Brazilian syncopation. Drift phonk is built on three elements: an 808 beat and bass, a lead (often an 808 cowbell), and dark atmosphere; usually 120 to 140 BPM. Brazilian phonk fuses drift phonk's harsh sounds with baile funk's vocal chops and tamborzão groove.
**Suno starter:** `Brazilian phonk, 130 BPM, tamborzão hand-drum groove, hard kick, distorted 808 cowbell lead, dark atmosphere, crowd chant.`

## Ideas for WaveWarZ rounds

Each round could be one genre, so the lore's characters or crews each own a sound:

- **Entrance theme:** trance build into a club drop.
- **Battle:** Jersey club or dubstep half-time drop.
- **Comeback:** drum and bass, the fastest track.
- **Villain / rival crew:** drift phonk, cowbell lead.
- **Victory party:** baile funk or UK garage.
- **Aftermath / cool-down:** amapiano.

When Max's lore bible (ORD-601) lands, match each chapter to one of these.
